Types of employer

Public

Councils, leisure trusts and government agencies. They run local pools, pitches and sports centres, and set national policy.

Non-profit

Governing bodies and charities, such as the Scottish FA, Scottish Rugby and Scottish Sports Futures. They grow participation, develop coaches and support performance sport.

Commercial

Clubs, brands, broadcasters, gyms, agencies and technology companies. They earn their income from sport.

Check employers directly

Many sport jobs are only advertised on the employer's own site or on LinkedIn. Choose a few employers you like and check their jobs pages every couple of weeks.

Getting hired

It starts with employability: how ready you are to get a job, do it well and progress your career.

Being employable

Having the skills, experience and qualities employers want.

Getting hired

Showing an employer you have them.

Four questions every employer is asking

  1. Can you do the job?Skills, knowledge and qualifications.
  2. Can you prove it?Experience, and examples of what you achieved.
  3. Will you be good to work with?Reliable, clear and willing to learn.
  4. Can you show all that?In your CV, your application and your interview.

    No wrong path

    There is more than one way into a career in sport, and changing direction is normal.

    You don't need to know exactly what you want to do yet. Most people don't. University, college, an apprenticeship or going straight into work can all lead to a career in sport.

    If your results were not what you hoped for, you still have options: clearing, college courses that lead into a degree, and jobs that don't ask for Highers at all.

    If you move away from sport later, what you learned comes with you. Teamwork, discipline and coping with setbacks are valued in every industry.

Second, if you allow it, Google Analytics, which sets its own cookies to count visits and work out which pages get used.
